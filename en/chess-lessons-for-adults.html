FIRST LESSON

First, I want to understand how you see your own game

Adult students can usually explain quite clearly what they enjoy about chess, where they feel they have problems and what they want to achieve through training.

01

02

We can play a game together or start by analyzing some of your recent games. This helps me see how you make decisions in practice.

03

04

I look at calculation, positional understanding, opening preparation, recurring mistakes and habits that may be holding you back.

05

We build a training plan

Gradually, I develop a complete picture of your game. From there, I identify the main areas to work on and build an individual training program around your goals.

EVERYONE HAS THEIR OWN REASON

Sometimes it all starts with wanting a rematch

People decide to take chess more seriously for all kinds of reasons. Some have loved the game for years and finally want to understand it on a deeper level. Others return to chess after a long break.

And sometimes the reason is much simpler: you started losing to your own child, lost a friendly game to a friend, or once made a bet that you could learn to play chess well.

I have seen many different stories. But in the end, they all have one thing in common: you enjoy chess and want to become a stronger player.

So it does not really matter what made you decide to take chess seriously. If you want to understand the game better and feel more confident at the board, I will be happy to help you get there.

INDIVIDUAL TRAINING PROGRAM

We focus on what is holding you back

Every chess player has different strengths and weaknesses, so I do not use the same training program for everyone. The main areas of work are determined after getting to know you, analyzing your games and understanding your goals.

Opening repertoire

We build an opening repertoire that suits your playing style. If necessary, we can create it almost from scratch or improve and restructure your existing repertoire.

Tactics and calculation

We work on recognizing tactical opportunities, calculating variations more deeply and making decisions based on concrete analysis rather than guesswork.

Strategy and middlegame

We study plans, typical positions, weaknesses and piece activity, helping you better understand what is happening on the board after the opening.

Endgames

We study important endgames and learn how to convert advantages, defend difficult positions and make more confident decisions in the final stage of the game.

Game analysis

We analyze your games, identify recurring mistakes and find the specific reasons that may be preventing you from improving.

Tournament preparation

If you compete in tournaments, we work on your form, opening repertoire, preparation for specific opponents and psychological readiness for your games.

TOURNAMENT PREPARATION

We prepare not only for the tournament, but for specific games

If you regularly compete in tournaments, tournament preparation becomes a separate part of our work.

We analyze your games, work on your opening repertoire, identify weak areas and decide what deserves special attention before your next event.

During a tournament, we can prepare for specific opponents. After each game, we can analyze the key decisions and, if necessary, adjust the preparation for the following rounds.

Psychological resilience is also an important part of this work. Mistakes, losses and poor decisions should not simply be forgotten — we need to understand why they happened and avoid carrying one bad game into the next.

01

Opponent preparation

We analyze the opponent's playing style and possible opening repertoire when enough information is available.

02

Game analysis

After the game, we analyze the key decisions, mistakes and missed opportunities.

03

Psychological preparation

We work on handling nerves, maintaining concentration, dealing with results and recovering mentally after a difficult game.

04

Adjustments during the tournament

We use the games you have already played to understand what should be adjusted before the next rounds.

RESULTS

Progress looks different for everyone

Some students want to play more confidently with friends or online, others want to understand their mistakes, while some are preparing for tournaments and aiming to reach a new level in their game.

01

Better understanding of the game

Instead of playing out of habit or simply guessing, you begin to understand the position: what is happening on the board, what plans are available and why one move may be better than another.

02

Fewer recurring mistakes

By analyzing your own games, we identify recurring mistakes and gradually turn them into specific areas that can be improved through systematic training.

03

Improvement at your own level

For an amateur player, this may mean playing with greater confidence. For a stronger player, it may mean serious work on specific areas of the game and preparation for competitions.

For me, progress is not only about rating points. What matters is that you understand your own decisions better, repeat the same mistakes less often and gradually become a more independent chess player.

WORK BETWEEN LESSONS

Knowledge should become a habit

One explanation is usually not enough. Important chess principles need to be repeated, applied in games and gradually turned into habits that you can use independently.

That is why my students hear some rules and ideas from me again and again. This repetition is intentional: my goal is to make sure that when the right moment comes during a game, you remember the principle and apply it without your coach's help.

Between lessons, I give individual homework assignments and review them. The results help us understand what has already been learned well and which topics we need to return to.

If necessary, I create a personal Lichess study where we can keep positions, variations, opening material and assignments. Zoom lessons can also be recorded so that you can return to a difficult explanation or position later.

01

02

We return to completed assignments, analyze mistakes and repeat important ideas until they are truly understood and become part of your game.

03

REGULARITY

Consistent training brings better results than occasional lessons

Chess cannot be learned in one or two lessons. New knowledge needs to be reinforced through games, mistakes need to be revisited and individual ideas gradually need to become reliable skills.

That is why regular lessons are the main format of my work. I usually recommend training at least once a week.

If your goal is faster improvement or serious tournament preparation, we can work together twice a week or more.

For regular students, I reserve a specific time in my schedule so that lessons become part of a consistent training process.

You can start with a trial lesson. After that, we will choose the most suitable lesson length, frequency and training plan for you.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

About the lessons

Answers to the most common questions before getting started.

Can I start learning chess from scratch?

Yes. I work with complete beginners as well as adults who have been playing chess for years and want to organize their knowledge and improve their understanding of the game.

Are the lessons suitable for experienced players?

Yes. For stronger players, our work can include detailed game analysis, opening preparation, calculation, strategy, endgames and tournament preparation.

How do online chess lessons work?

Lessons are held via Zoom and Lichess. We can play games, analyze your games, solve positions and work with training materials prepared specifically for you.

Do you give homework?

Yes. Assignments are selected individually according to your current goals and are always reviewed. If necessary, I can also create a personal Lichess study for your independent training.

How often should I take lessons?

For regular training, one lesson per week is usually a good starting point. If your goal is faster improvement or serious tournament preparation, we can work together twice a week or more.

Can I book individual lessons without a subscription?

Yes. One-off lessons are available, but the price per lesson is significantly higher than the price of a lesson within a package.

For systematic improvement, I recommend regular training. New knowledge needs to be reinforced, applied in games and gradually turned into reliable skills.
